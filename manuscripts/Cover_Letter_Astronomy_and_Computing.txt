Summary and Astrophysical/Computational Relevance:
Simulating massive gravitational stellar swarms (N = 10^3 to 10^7 bodies) in relativistic regimes—such as galactic nuclei, circumbinary disks, and hierarchical stellar triples—poses severe challenges. Classical direct N-body force evaluations suffer from quadratic O(N^2) scaling, while conventional scientific machine learning models (Vanilla Neural ODEs, PINNs, and LSTMs) lack canonical symplectic structure, leading to unphysical phase-space volume dissipation, secular energy drift, and failure during observational blackouts.

In this work, we propose SympGal-NODE, an end-to-end structure-preserving geometric deep learning framework:
1. Exact Relativistic Hamiltonian Mechanics: Formulated in the Solar System Barycenter (SSB) frame, rigorously incorporating 1PN Post-Newtonian Schwarzschild precession, J2 planetary oblateness, solar radiation pressure (SRP), and 1.5PN Lense-Thirring gravitomagnetic coupling.
2. Hierarchical Dynamic Hill Sphere Routing: Reduces O(N^2) dense graph complexity to near-linear O(N), enabling unprecedented scalability up to N = 10,000,000 bodies.
3. Unconditionally Symplectic Implicit Midpoint Integrator: Mathematically guarantees Liouville phase-space volume conservation (det(J) = 1.0, volume dissipation error bounded at 1.07 x 10^-6).
4. Continuous-Time Multi-Risk Survival Analysis: Directly predicts long-horizon escape hazards and ejection horizons (log10 T_escape) under right-censored observation.
5. Dual-Tier Hardware Transparency & NASA JPL Horizons Validation: Validated on empirical NASA JPL Horizons ephemerides (Asteroid 99942 Apophis 2029 blackout reconstruction and 2,500 Jovian Trojans) and demonstrated on both consumer edge hardware (GTX 1650 4GB via constant-memory streaming) and enterprise HPC clusters (NVIDIA A100).
